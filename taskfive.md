## Milestone 1 - ChatGPT : Increase the Usage of Voice Input on the ChatGPT Mobile App

### Solution to ChatGPT Mapping Business Outcome to Product Outcome: Increase the Usage of Voice Input on the ChatGPT Mobile App

#### Task 5 - Understand why Increasing Voice Usage could be Valuable for ChatGPT - and Outline potential Product Outcomes.

Increasing voice usage could be valuable for ChatGPT because **voice changes both the frequency and the kinds of moments in which people can use AI**. It isn't just another input method—it can expand the product's addressable use cases.

**1. It increases usage frequency**

Typing requires a deliberate interaction. Voice works in moments where typing is inconvenient:

- Walking or commuting
- Cooking
- Exercising
- Brainstorming
- Driving, where appropriate and safely
- Multitasking
- Learning or practicing a language

So voice can move ChatGPT from "**I open the app when I have a question**" toward "**I talk to ChatGPT throughout my day.**"

**Potential Outcome:** higher sessions per user, longer engagement, and stronger retention.

**2. It lowers the interaction cost**

Typing is relatively slow. Speaking is much faster for many users, particularly for long or complicated thoughts.

That makes voice valuable for prompts such as:

"I've got a meeting tomorrow with the product team, and I'm worried about these three things..."

That's much easier to say than type.

**Potential Outcome:** more complex prompts and richer context → potentially better task completion.

**3. It creates new use cases**

Voice enables experiences that are awkward through text:

- Language practice
- Interview practice
- Tutoring
- Brainstorming
- Coaching
- Storytelling
- Hands-free Q&A
- Real-time explanations
- Conversational planning

This is particularly important because some of these are **high-frequency behaviors**, rather than occasional information searches.

**Potential Outcome:** expansion into new user segments and use cases.

**4. Voice makes ChatGPT feel more like an assistant**

There's a psychological difference between:

"I'm using an AI tool."

and:

"I'm talking to my assistant."

Natural back-and-forth conversation - with interruption, clarification and spoken responses—makes the latter possible.

That's strategically important because ChatGPT is competing not only with other AI apps, but increasingly with the **traditional smartphone assistant experience.**

**Potential Outcome:** stronger product habit and greater daily relevance.

**5. Voice + multimodality is much more powerful than voice alone**

The really interesting opportunity isn't:

**voice** → **voice**

It's:

**voice** + **camera** + **screen** + **text** + **web** + **memory**

For example:

"What am I looking at?"

📷 Camera

"Explain that part."

👉 User points to something

"Okay, now help me fix it."

That's a completely different interaction model from a conventional chatbot.

**Potential Outcome:** higher task completion and differentiation from competitors.

### Potential Product Outcomes

I'd organize the business impact into five buckets:

| Product outcome             | Mechanism                             | What you'd measure        |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Higher engagement**       | More convenient interaction           | Voice sessions/user       |
| **Higher retention**        | Daily conversational habit            | D7/D30 retention          |
| **New use cases**           | Hands-free + conversational scenarios | Voice task categories     |
| **Higher task completion**  | Richer context + multimodality        | Successful task rate      |
| **Greater differentiation** | More natural assistant experience     | Preference vs competitors |

There's also a sixth, longer-term outcome:

**Voice can become the gateway to agentic behavior.**

Imagine:

"Find me three good options for dinner tomorrow, compare them, and remind me what I need to know before I go."

The user doesn't need to formulate a structured command. They can simply **talk through the objective**.

ChatGPT can then research, reason, and potentially execute parts of the workflow.

That's a powerful product trajectory:

**Voice** → **conversation** → **intent** → **agent** → **outcome**

### But there's a Critical Caveat

I wouldn't optimize for **voice usage alone**.

A user speaking for 20 minutes isn't necessarily more valuable than someone completing a task in two minutes.

The north-star question should be:

**Does voice help users accomplish things they wouldn't - or couldn't as easily - accomplish through text?**

So I'd track **quality of voice usage**, not just volume:

- Voice-to-task-completion rate
- Repeat voice usage
- Voice-assisted successful outcomes
- Time to useful answer
- Interruptions / corrections
- Voice → text transitions
- Voice → image transitions
- Voice → agent/workflow transitions
- Retention of voice users versus comparable text-only users

### The Strategic Thesis

**Increasing voice usage is valuable if voice becomes a new interaction layer for ChatGPT - not merely a faster keyboard.**

The ultimate product outcome would be a shift from:

"**Open ChatGPT and type a prompt.**"

to:

"**When I need help, I just talk to ChatGPT.**"

That could substantially increase **frequency, breadth of use cases, habit formation, and the amount of real-world context ChatGPT receives**, while creating a natural entry point into multimodal and agentic experiences.
